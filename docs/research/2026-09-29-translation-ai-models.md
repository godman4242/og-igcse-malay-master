Bergamot quality (MEASURED, FLORES-200+ test set, from the registry): en->ms chrF 68.95, COMET22 0.881; ms->en chrF 65.02, COMET22 0.866. These are the "tiny" models. I found NO published Google-vs-Bergamot or Google-vs-LLM number for Malay specifically, so "better than Google" cannot be proven.

Is anything BETTER than Google for Malay? No source I found shows it. General benchmark papers say Google Translate beats GPT-4-turbo on FLORES-200 and open LLMs lag supervised NMT, mostly on low-resource languages (https://arxiv.org/html/2502.02481v2, https://arxiv.org/pdf/2404.13813); none isolates Malay. The one Malay-relevant risk I could not test is Indonesian leakage (Indonesian words shown where Malaysian Malay is correct, e.g. the kamu/awak issue); that is a defect to test with a small hand-checked word list, not something a source settles.

Is anything cheaper than gtx (free, no key)? Yes: Bergamot on-device costs nothing per call, has no quota, works offline and does not depend on an unofficial Google endpoint. Trade-off: tiny models are weaker than Google/LLM on long or idiomatic sentences, ~34 MB download for both directions, and you must bundle the WASM engine yourself (not turnkey).

### Recommendation (section 1)
Use a layered approach. (1) Single words: keep answering from the app's own dictionary first (a hand-verified source, and the Malay-fluency gate). (2) Sentences, default with no key: ship Bergamot `ms-en` and `en-ms` (lazy-loaded, cached) as the primary free/no-key path, because it is the only candidate that is truly free, quota-free, private, Malay-supported and has published Malay metrics. (3) Fallback order: Bergamot -> gtx (keep, but treat as best-effort since it is unofficial) -> a BYOK Gemini flash-lite call when the student has a key and wants a better sentence. Do not add DeepL (no free API) or Azure (card). Do not rely on Chrome's Translator API (no `ms`). Before swapping gtx out, run a 50-sentence hand-graded bake-off (gtx vs Bergamot vs Gemini) on Malaysian-register sentences; nothing found proves Bergamot is better than Google, only cheaper and independent. Confirm the Bergamot model licence (the Mozilla models repo says see LICENSE; I did not read it) before bundling.

## 2. AI models (late Sep 2026)

### 2a. Gemini lineup and free tier
Current model IDs per the official models page: gemini-3.8-flash, gemini-3.7-flash, gemini-3.6-flash, gemini-3.5-flash, gemini-3.5-flash-lite, gemini-3.1-flash-lite (stable); gemini-3.1-pro-preview and gemini-3-flash-preview (preview). 2.5 models are "limited to prior users"; 2.0 Flash / Flash-Lite are shut down. https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/models
Free tier per the official pricing page: free of charge for 3.8 Flash, 3.7 Flash, 3.5 Flash, 3.5 Flash-Lite and others; NOT free: 3.1 Pro Preview, Gemma 4, image/video/music models. https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/pricing
Requests/day: Google no longer publishes numbers in the docs (rate-limits page just points to the AI Studio dashboard: https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/rate-limits). A measured post (read the quota out of 429 errors, key created that morning, no billing): full Flash models = 20 requests/day, Flash-Lite variants = 500/day, per project not per key, resets midnight Pacific. https://dev.to/romeroyang/geminis-free-tier-measured-20-requests-a-day-and-google-no-longer-publishes-the-number-4gf2 . Independent confirmation from a project that hit 20/day on gemini-3.6-flash and switched to gemini-3.5-flash-lite (merged 2026-09-15): https://github.com/Justinbiahoi-coding/ai-career-skill-coach/pull/9
Older blogs claim 1,500 RPD for Flash; they describe 2.x models and are stale (e.g. https://tokenmix.ai/blog/gemini-api-free-tier-limits, dated April 2026 and covering 2.5/2.0).
Paid price for scale (OpenRouter live API, per token): gemini-3.5-flash-lite $0.30 in / $2.50 out per M; gemini-3.8-flash $0.75 / $3.75. https://openrouter.ai/api/v1/models (MEASURED)
Best quality-per-cost for a tutor/grader: gemini-3.5-flash-lite. Flash 20 RPD is useless per student; flash-lite's 500 RPD/project is the only Gemini free option with usable volume. No Malay-specific evidence found comparing flash-lite to flash, so quality on Malay grading is unmeasured (matches your existing plan to run the ai-tier eval).
Data caveat: free-tier Gemini data may be used by Google to improve models (https://tokenmix.ai/blog/gemini-api-free-tier-limits, secondary source; confirm in Gemini terms before sending student text).

### 2c. "jev"

### 2d. Strongest free options for Malay
Malay evidence I could get:
- SEA-HELM (AI Singapore) leaderboard, updated 2026-09-18, averages 7 languages including Malay; "SEA Overall" top four visible: Gemma 4 (31B), Qwen 3.5 (122B-A10B), Qwen 3.6 (27B), SEA-LION v4.5 (Qwen 27B). Malay-only column NOT extractable (SPA behind tRPC). https://leaderboard.sea-lion.ai/ . SEA-LION v4.8 report confirms Malay was added to SEA-HELM tasks (https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.18310).
- MalayMMLU (24,213 Malaysian school-syllabus questions, Year 1-Form 5, EMNLP 2024): the 18 models tested were old; GPT-4 was best. No 2026 numbers found. https://aclanthology.org/2024.findings-emnlp.36.pdf ; mesolitica Malay leaderboard exists but results not retrievable: https://github.com/mesolitica/llm-benchmarks
Free availability (MEASURED from the OpenRouter live model list, priced 0/0, text): google/gemma-4-31b-it:free, google/gemma-4-26b-a4b-it:free, qwen/qwen3.8-27b:free, nvidia/nemotron-3-ultra-550b-a55b:free, nvidia/nemotron-3-super-120b-a12b:free, openrouter/free (router), plus smaller/niche ones. OpenRouter free limits: under 10 credits = 20 req/min and 50 req/DAY; 10+ credits = 1,000/day. https://openrouter.ai/docs/api-reference/limits . Rankings above make Gemma 4 31B and Qwen 3.x 27B the best-evidenced free models for SEA languages.
Groq free (no card): gpt-oss-120b/20b and Qwen 3.x 27B at 30 RPM, 1,000 RPD, 8K TPM per org (docs: https://console.groq.com/docs/rate-limits ; model list from a blog: https://klymentiev.com/blog/groq-pricing). Llama left free tier 2026-08-16 (blog claim, unverified against docs).
Cerebras: official docs show a Free Trial of $5 credit that EXPIRES after 30 days, models gpt-oss-120b and qwen-3.8-27b at 5 RPM (https://inference-docs.cerebras.ai/support/rate-limits). Blogs still say "1M tokens/day free"; docs win -> not a durable free tier.
Mistral free tier: could not verify (docs URL 404); left out.

Structural point: a server proxy uses ONE key, so every student shares one quota (OpenRouter 50/day, Groq 1,000/day and 200K tokens/day per org, Gemini flash-lite 500/day per project). A free no-key default can only serve a handful of students per day; BYOK scales because the quota is per student.

### Recommendation (section 2)
(i) Server-proxy default (free, no user key): OpenRouter `google/gemma-4-31b-it:free` first (highest-ranked on SEA-HELM overall among free models), fall through to `qwen/qwen3.8-27b:free`, then `openrouter/free`; discover slugs at runtime as your CLAUDE.md already requires, and add Groq (Qwen 3.x 27B) as a second provider to double the pool. Cap and cache aggressively (50 req/day on OpenRouter without credits is the real ceiling) and keep the free confidence-gated expert system as the first tier. (ii) Best BYOK: student's own Gemini key with `gemini-3.5-flash-lite` (500 RPD, no card, per-student quota, best cost/quality of the Gemini line); Groq key with Qwen 3.x 27B as the alternative; Ollama for offline. Avoid GLM (no Malay evidence; the only good one is paid), Jev (not a text model). Verify with your ai-tier eval on Malay grading before you promote any of these; the leaderboard rank is a multi-language average, not Malay grading skill.

### Recommendation (section 3)
Link to the three official Cambridge pages (and specimen PDFs) from a "Real exam papers" screen; do not host, embed, OCR-and-store, or reproduce past-paper questions or mark schemes, and do not link to third-party mirrors. Build all in-app practice from original content written to the specimen format. Use PRPM and Wiktionary only as manual verification sources for glosses (PRPM: look, don't copy; Wiktionary: copy only if you accept CC BY-SA on the result). If you want to embed even one specimen paper, email Cambridge via the permission form first.
